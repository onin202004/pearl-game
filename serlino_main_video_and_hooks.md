## MAIN VIDEO SCRIPT (~6:30–7:00)

**Cast:** FRANK (58, divorced 3 yrs, runs a hardware store) · LINDA (mid-50s, the date) · GUS (68, fishing buddy, looks sharp) · JOANNE (Frank's sister) · DENNIS (Joanne's husband) · WAITER · PHARMACY CLERK · GYM KID · BARISTA · BARBER

---

**SC 1 — RESTAURANT, NIGHT** *(cold open; hooks replace/precede this)*

**LINDA:** I'm going to be honest, because I think you'd want me to be. Your profile said 58. Sitting across from you… you look tired, Frank. Like a man who gave up. I already did that once. I can't do it again. I'm sorry.
*(She leaves. Long beat. Waiter approaches.)*
**WAITER:** Will the lady be returning, sir?
**FRANK:** No. But I'll still have the steak.
**WAITER:** Of course. *(beat)* Drinks are on me tonight.
**FRANK (VO):** First date since the divorce. Three years. And I couldn't argue with a single word she said.

**SC 2 — FRANK'S KITCHEN, PHONE CALL**

**LINDA (phone):** Frank, it's Linda. I'm sorry for how I said it. Not for what I said.
**FRANK:** You don't owe me a call.
**LINDA:** I think I do. I'm at my sister's in Portland all summer. I'm back September 6th. If you want coffee… the Blue Door, ten o'clock.
**FRANK:** That's ten weeks out.
**LINDA:** I know. Come if you want to. I'd like it if you did.
**FRANK:** *(after she hangs up)* Then I've got ten weeks.
**FRANK (VO):** I started the next morning. Ten weeks. Like a mission with a date on it.

**SC 3 — PHARMACY COUNTER**

**CLERK:** With the senior discount, that comes to—
**FRANK:** I'm 58.
**CLERK:** Oh. That's 65 and up… it's sixteen forty, then. Sorry.

**SC 4 — BATHROOM, MORNING** *(denial ritual: soap)*

*(Frank washes face with the same bar of soap he uses on hair and body.)*
**FRANK (VO):** Thirty years, one bar of soap. Hair, body, face. I was proud of that. Figured a man who doesn't fuss is a man who's fine.

**SC 5 — GYM**

**GYM KID:** Want a hand getting that set up, sir?
**FRANK:** I remember how. It's just been a while.

**SC 6 — WORK / STORE COUNTER**

**COWORKER:** Frank, honest question — you okay? You look like you haven't slept in a month.
**FRANK:** Allergies. Bad week. *(beat; VO)* I had an excuse for every bag under my eyes.

**SC 7 — BACKYARD BBQ**

**DENNIS:** Joanne says you're hitting the gym every morning now.
**FRANK:** Eighteen days straight.
**DENNIS:** Well, whatever it's doing, it ain't reached your face yet, buddy.
**NEIGHBOR LADY:** So how are you enjoying retirement, Frank?
**FRANK:** I'm not retired. I run a hardware store. I'm 58.
**NEIGHBOR LADY:** Oh God, I'm so sorry—
**FRANK:** It's all right. Happens a lot lately.

**SC 8 — BATHROOM MIRROR** *(gym trap)*

**FRANK (VO):** Eleven pounds down. And the mirror gave me nothing back. Worse, actually. Losing the weight made my face look hollow. Older. Like the frame was showing through.

**SC 9 — DRUGSTORE AISLE / BATHROOM** *(❌ montage — the "tried everything")*

**FRANK (VO):** So I tried the stuff. Sixty-dollar neck cream — looked shiny for an hour. That beef tallow everybody's rubbing on their face — greasy pillow, same face. Called a clinic about Botox. Six hundred bucks to freeze it in place.

**SC 10 — JOANNE'S PORCH, EVENING**

**JOANNE:** Frank, look at me a second. *(beat)* You've lost weight, but your face… you look worn out. Is everything okay?
**FRANK:** I'm fine, Jo.
**JOANNE:** You're not. And I hate that I can see it.
**FRANK (VO):** Seven weeks left. And she was going to sit across from the same tired man… and say it nicer this time.

**SC 11 — BOAT LAUNCH, MORNING** *(mentor + mechanism)*

**FRANK:** Gus, how do you look like that? You're ten years older than me, and you look ten years younger.
**GUS:** You asking for real?
**FRANK:** For real.
**GUS:** Three years ago my own wife told me I looked like I was melting off the bone. She wasn't wrong. *(beat)* What've you been putting on it?
**FRANK:** Cream from the drugstore. Tallow. Almost did Botox.
**GUS:** *(laughs)* The cream's silicone paint on dry rot. Shines the surface while the house sinks. Tallow's greasing the engine without turning the key. And Botox just freezes the beams — doesn't rebuild 'em.
**FRANK:** Rebuild what?
**GUS:** The frame. After fifty, that sheet of muscle holding up your jaw and neck — the platysma — loses its fat foundation. The collagen scaffolding under it starts to give. It's not your skin, Frank. It's the foundation settling.
**GUS:** This is what fixed mine. Serlino. One pump, twenty seconds before bed. Replaces everything — including that bar of soap.
**FRANK:** I'm not a skincare guy, Gus.
**GUS:** Neither am I. This isn't skincare. It's structural maintenance. You change the oil in your truck, don't you?
**FRANK:** *(beat)* Twenty seconds.
**GUS:** Give it ten weeks. Week one, don't expect a thing.

**SC 12 — GYM, WEEK 1**

**GYM KID:** You're still here every morning. That's rare. Most guys quit by week two.
**FRANK:** I've got a coffee date in six weeks.
**GUS (phone, VO):** How's week one?
**FRANK:** Skin feels tighter. Face still looks like a washed-up has-been.
**GUS:** I said ten weeks, not one. Keep pumping.

**SC 13 — COFFEE SHOP, WEEK 3** *(radar detection)*

*(Barista hands Frank his cup. Does a double-take. Writes her number on the sleeve.)*
**FRANK (VO):** Week three. First thing on this face that had moved in years. Lines easing off. And a girl at the coffee shop wrote her number on my cup. *(beat)* I didn't call her. But I walked out of there with my head held high.

**SC 14 — PORCH, WAITING ON THE BOX** *(optional — matches Forge 4:37)*

**FRANK (VO):** I'd ordered `[X]` more bottles. Box was two days out. I sat there thinking how quiet my life had gotten. Whole days I didn't say ten words to anybody. It felt good to be waiting on something again.

**SC 15 — PHARMACY COUNTER, WEEK 8**

**CLERK:** Sorry — you've been in before, right?
**FRANK:** Couple months back. You offered me the senior discount.
**CLERK:** That was *you?* *(beat)* No discount today. Sixteen forty.
**FRANK:** Sixteen forty's fine.

**SC 16 — BOAT LAUNCH, WEEK 10**

**GUS:** Look at that jaw. Ten weeks.
**FRANK:** The gym fixed the belt. Serlino fixed the frame.
**GUS:** Tomorrow, ten o'clock. Don't be late.
**FRANK:** I've been early for ten weeks.

**SC 17 — THE BLUE DOOR CAFÉ, 10 AM** *(payoff)*

**LINDA:** *(looks up from menu; double-take)* …Frank?
**FRANK:** Morning, Linda.
**LINDA:** I've thought about that dinner all summer.
**FRANK:** You were right, though. I was a man who gave up.
**LINDA:** You don't look like him anymore. You look like a man who came back.
**FRANK:** I'd like a second chance. A real dinner. My treat.
**LINDA:** Yes.
**LINDA:** You should know why it came out so sharp. My husband passed six years ago. His last year, he wore that exact face. A man who'd already left. I cried in my car after our dinner.
**FRANK:** Then I'm glad you said it.

**SC 18 — BBQ / BARBERSHOP** *(social proof)*

**DENNIS:** You said the gym hadn't reached my face. *(Frank: "This is what reached it.")* Joanne showed me the picture from the café. Give me one of those, I'm serious.
**FRANK:** Keep it. I bought `[X]`.
**BARBER:** *(holding mirror)* Okay, what'd you do?
**FRANK (VO):** Thirty years in that chair, and it's the first time he's asked.

**SC 19 — KITCHEN TABLE** *(risk reversal)*

**FRANK (VO):** I read the fine print first, because I don't trust anything. `[GUARANTEE — e.g., "X days or your money back"]`. Didn't need it.

**SC 20 — CTA**

**FRANK (VO):** Ten weeks after the worst dinner of my life, the same woman said yes. It's called Serlino. One pump, twenty seconds before bed. `[OFFER — e.g., "It's up to X% off right now"]`, and the link's right below.
**FRANK:** Ten weeks ago, I was a man who gave up. Not anymore.

## HOOK VARIATIONS (swap in at 0:00, 10–20s each)

Lahat ng hook ay nagtatapos sa iisang **bridge line**, tapos diretso sa SC 1:

> **"But that wasn't the worst thing anyone said to me that year."** → cut to SC 1 (Linda at dinner)

(Pareho ito sa primary text ng Forge: pharmacy clerk muna, tapos "that wasn't the worst thing...")

**H1 · Humiliation comedy — the trainer**
*Visual: Frank opens a bill at the kitchen counter, deadpan.*
> FRANK (VO): My wife left me for her personal trainer. The one I was paying eighty bucks an hour. I found out when he billed me for a session she skipped… because she was with him.
> → bridge

**H2 · Humiliation comedy — Rome**
*Visual: Rome café, Frank's best smile at a woman two tables over. She walks over, hand on his shoulder, turns to the waiter.*
> WOMAN: Does he need a doctor?
> FRANK (VO): First vacation in eleven years. That was my best smile.
> → bridge

**H3 · Universal nightmare — "sweetheart"**
*Visual: diner booth. Waitress refills coffee.*
> WAITRESS: More coffee, sweetheart? *(she pats his hand)*
> FRANK (VO): You know that voice. The one you use on somebody's grandpa. I'm 58.
> → bridge

**H4 · Pharmacy (Forge's own primary-text hook, Serlino'd)**
*Visual: SC 3 moved to the top.*
> CLERK: With the senior discount, that comes to— / FRANK: I'm 58.
> FRANK (VO): I paid my sixteen forty and kept my hand steady.
> → bridge

**H5 · Mirror denial — the soap**
*Visual: close-up, one cracked bar of Irish Spring-style green soap. Frank lathers hair, then face.*
> FRANK (VO): Thirty years. One bar of soap. Hair, body, face. I was proud of that — no fancy crap. Then my barber held up the hand mirror to show me the back… and I saw my neck from the side.
> → bridge

**H6 · Mirror denial — the gym trap**
*Visual: reunion name tag "FRANK — Class of '86". Old friend grabs his arm.*
> FRIEND: *(whispering)* Jesus, Frank. Are you sick?
> FRANK (VO): I'd dropped eleven pounds for that reunion. Turns out losing the weight made my face look older.
> → bridge

**H7 · Outsmart the system**
*Visual: Frank's bathroom shelf — drugstore cream, tub of beef tallow, Botox clinic quote on the fridge.*
> FRANK (VO): Sixty-dollar neck cream. Beef tallow. A six-hundred-dollar Botox quote. Took a 68-year-old guy at a boat launch to tell me why none of it was ever going to work.
> → bridge *(or jump straight to SC 11 for a shorter cut)*

**H8 · The 10-week clock**
*Visual: calendar on the fridge, "SEPT 6 — 10AM" circled. Frank crosses off day one.*
> FRANK (VO): Ten weeks. That's how long I had before a woman who walked out on me sat across from me again.
> → cut straight to SC 1
